7-Stage DistilBERT Thematic Processing Pipeline
How raw text is transformed into outcome probabilities and themes
1. Standardized Sequence Construction: Joins Headline | Position | Company | About | Skills into a unified semantic sequence.
2. WordPiece Subword Tokenization: Maps text into 30,522-vocab subword IDs prepended with [CLS] (101) and appended with [SEP] (102).
3. Dual Embedding Lookup: Combines Token Embeddings + Positional Embeddings into a $(1 \times L \times 768)$ dense tensor.
4. 6-Layer Multi-Head Self-Attention: 12 attention heads ($d_k = 64$) link intent modifiers (e.g., "seeking", "aspiring") directly to role nouns.
5. [CLS] Pooled Representation: Extracts the 768-dimensional contextual summary vector at index 0.
6. Linear Logits & Softmax: Projects $\mathbb{R}^{768} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^3$ logits and computes Softmax probabilities $P(y=k|\mathbf{x})$.
7. Thematic Coding & TF-IDF Clustering: Combines codebook phrase weights, DistilBERT priors, and K-Means clustering.
Taglish Linguistic Processing & Morphological Adapter
Mixed English-Tagalog qualitative handling, affix stripping, and bilingual stopwords
1. Taglish Affix Normalizer: Strips verbal and nominal affixes (nag-, mag-, pag-, pagka-, na-, ma-, i-, -in-, -an) to match root governance keywords (e.g., "nag-cramming" → "cramming", "kinulang" → "kulang").
2. Bilingual SGLG Codebook: Core themes populated with verified Tagalog governance terminology (e.g., "pamumuno", "ugnayan", "kulang sa tao", "tuloy-tuloy", "ramdam ng tao").
3. Comprehensive Tagalog Stop Words: Prunes 80+ grammatical particles (ang, mga, sa, ng, dahil, pero, lang, po, natin) so TF-IDF and K-Means only capture substantive thematic concepts.
4. Emergent Code Fallback: Statements lacking predefined codebook triggers are automatically assigned salient candidate codes ([Emergent]) and labeled as Unassigned / Needs Review for qualitative researcher verification.
Thesis Defense Blueprint: DistilBERT NLP & Thematic Analysis FAQ
Methodological justification for committee panels and academic defense
Q1: Does DistilBERT "understand context" to generate codes?
Yes, but as an Encoder, not a Generative LLM. DistilBERT uses 12 bidirectional self-attention heads to understand the semantic context of each statement. However, in this system, it is an Encoder-Classifier ($[CLS] \rightarrow \text{Logits}$), mapping context into 3 governance trajectories (Sustained, Constrained, Deficient). Codes and narratives are assembled by the CAQDAS engine using this trajectory to contextualize matched empirical quotes.
Q2: Why not use a generative model (like ChatGPT or Gemini) to generate codes?
To eliminate AI hallucinations and ensure auditability. In academic qualitative research, generative LLMs often invent synthetic quotes or hallucinate themes not in the raw data. This hybrid architecture guarantees that 100% of quotes are authentic verbatim participant statements traceable to informant IDs (e.g., LCE-03, MPDC-02).
Q3: How does DistilBERT handle Taglish if it has an English vocabulary?
Via Anchor Nouns & Morphological Normalization. SGLG transcripts in Philippine local governance are anchored in English operational terms ("budget", "ordinance", "cramming", "assessment"). The Taglish Linguistic Normalizer strips Tagalog affixes (e.g., "nag-cramming" → "cramming", "kinulang" → "kulang") before tokenization, allowing DistilBERT to classify the trajectory accurately.
